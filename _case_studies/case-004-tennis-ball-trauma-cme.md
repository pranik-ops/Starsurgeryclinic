## Diagnosis & Medical Taxonomy

**Primary Condition:** Traumatic subhyaloid hemorrhage with secondary cystoid macular edema (Left Eye, OS).

**ICD-10 Codes:**
- `H43.10` — Vitreous hemorrhage
- `H35.81` — Cystoid macular edema
- `S05.12XA` — Contusion of eyeball and orbital tissues, left eye

**MeSH Terms:** Eye Injuries, Blunt; Macular Edema; Retinal Hemorrhage; Angiogenesis Inhibitors

---

## Baseline Presentation

**Patient Profile:** 36-year-old female presented 2 days after acute blunt trauma from a tennis ball to the left eye (OS).

**Chief Complaints:**
- Black spot in front of left eye
- Blurring of vision in the same eye
- Dense central scotoma and vision loss

**Initial Evaluation:**
- Visual Acuity (OS): Finger Counting at 2 meters (FC @ 2m)
- Dilated Fundus Examination: Subhyaloid hemorrhage (blood layer between retina and vitreous)

**Diagnostic Imaging:**
- **Optical Coherence Tomography (OCT):** 
  - Confirmed subhyaloid hemorrhage
  - Severe intraretinal fluid accumulation
  - Cystoid macular edema (fluid pockets in the macula)
  - Loss of normal foveal contour

---

## Treatment Intervention

### Medical Management
- **Systemic Therapy:** Intravenous Mannitol (osmotic diuretic) to reduce intraocular fluid and accelerate hemorrhage clearance
- **Targeted Retinal Therapy:** Intravitreal Anti-VEGF (Vascular Endothelial Growth Factor inhibitor) injection into the left eye
  - Goal: Arrest microvascular permeability and resolve macular swelling
  - Avoids need for major open surgery

---

## Objective Endpoint Outcome

**Follow-Up Interval:** 3 Weeks Post-Treatment

**Visual Acuity (OS):**
- Baseline: Finger Counting at 2m
- Follow-Up: **6/12** (significant improvement)
- Status: Continuing to improve

**Clinical Status:**
- Patient symptomatically better
- Reduced cystoid macular edema on imaging
- Vision restoration in affected eye ongoing

---

## Key Clinical Takeaway for Patients

Sports injuries to the eye that cause sudden vision loss and a dark spot in your field of vision should be evaluated immediately. Modern non-surgical treatments like anti-VEGF injections combined with medications to reduce fluid can restore your vision significantly, even after serious blunt trauma. Most patients see meaningful improvement within 3 weeks and continue recovering with appropriate follow-up care.
