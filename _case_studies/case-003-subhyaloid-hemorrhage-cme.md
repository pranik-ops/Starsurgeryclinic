## Diagnosis & Medical Taxonomy

**Primary Condition:** Traumatic subhyaloid hemorrhage with secondary cystoid macular edema (Left Eye, OS).

**ICD-10 Codes:**
- `H43.10` — Vitreous hemorrhage
- `H35.81` — Cystoid macular edema
- `S05.12XA` — Contusion of eyeball and orbital tissues, left eye

**MeSH Terms:** Eye Injuries, Blunt; Macular Edema; Retinal Hemorrhage; Angiogenesis Inhibitors

---

## Baseline Presentation

**Patient Profile:** 36-year-old female presenting 48 hours following acute blunt trauma from a high-velocity tennis ball to the left eye (OS), reporting a dense central scotoma and profound blurring.

**Pre-Treatment Baseline Vision:** 
- Visual Acuity: Finger Counting at 2 meters (FC @ 2m)

**Diagnostic Imaging:**
- **Dilated Fundus Exam:** Premacular/subhyaloid hemorrhage confirmed
- **Spectral-Domain OCT (SD-OCT):** 
  - Severe intraretinal fluid accumulation
  - Loss of normal foveal contour
  - Consistent with traumatic cystoid macular edema

---

## Targeted Medical Intervention

### Pharmacotherapy
- **Systemic osmotic diuresis:** I/V Mannitol 20% to accelerate clearance and control intraocular fluid dynamics

### Targeted Retinal Therapy
- **Intravitreal Anti-VEGF Injection:** Vascular Endothelial Growth Factor inhibitor administered under sterile operative conditions
- **Goal:** Arrest microvascular permeability and resolve macular swelling without major open surgery

---

## Objective Endpoint Outcome

**Follow-Up Interval:** 3 Weeks Post-Injection

**Visual Acuity (OS):**
- Improved from Finger Counting at 2m → **6/12** (ongoing recovery)

**Objective Imaging (OCT):**
- Marked anatomical reduction of central subfield thickness (CST)
- Substantial clearing of subhyaloid blood layers
- No vitreoretinal traction or choroidal rupture

---

## Key Clinical Takeaway for Patients

Blunt sports trauma causing immediate vision loss can often be **reversed without major open surgery** if diagnostic OCT imaging and intravitreal anti-VEGF therapy are initiated promptly after injury.
