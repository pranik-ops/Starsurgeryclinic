## Diagnosis & Medical Taxonomy

**Primary Condition:** Full-thickness corneoscleral laceration with iris incarceration (Right Eye); secondary traumatic cataract.

**ICD-10 Codes:**
- `S05.31XA` — Ocular laceration with tissue prolapse, initial encounter
- `H26.10` — Traumatic cataract, unspecified

**MeSH Terms:** Eye Injuries, Penetrating; Corneal Perforation; Cataract, Traumatic; Lens Implantation, Intraocular

---

## Baseline Presentation & Emergency Triage

**Patient Profile:** 8-year-old male presented to the 24/7 Emergency Trauma Unit within 2 hours of sharp penetrating trauma to the right eye (OD).

**Initial Evaluation:** Full-thickness corneoscleral perforation at the 7 o'clock meridian with acute iris prolapse/incarceration and flat anterior chamber.

**Diagnostics:** 
- Orbital X-Ray: Ruled out radiopaque foreign bodies
- Ocular B-Scan Ultrasonography: Ruled out metallic intraocular foreign bodies (IOFB)

---

## Surgical Intervention & Phased Strategy

### Phase 1: Immediate Emergency Repair
- **Procedure:** Exploration, repositioning/resection of viable uveal tissue, and anatomical watertight closure of the corneoscleral wound
- **Technique:** 10-0 nylon sutures under general anesthesia
- **Goal:** Restore globe integrity and prevent vision loss from open eye

### Phase 2: Secondary Reconstruction (4 Weeks Post-Injury)
- **Timing:** Following complete quiescence of anterior chamber inflammation
- **Procedure:** Clear corneal phaco-aspiration with:
  - Primary posterior capsulotomy
  - In-the-bag Posterior Chamber Intraocular Lens (PCIOL) implantation
- **Goal:** Restore optical clarity and functional vision

---

## Objective Endpoint Outcome

**Follow-Up Interval:** 1 Month Post-Secondary IOL Implantation

**Visual Acuity (OD):**
- Unaided Visual Acuity (UCVA): 6/18
- Best-Corrected Visual Acuity (BCVA): 6/6 (partial)

**Anatomical Status:**
- Stable, centered PCIOL
- Deep and quiet anterior chamber
- Intact tectonic integrity of repaired corneoscleral junction
- No secondary glaucoma

---

## Key Clinical Takeaway for Patients

Penetrating pediatric eye injuries require **immediate tectonic closure** to preserve the globe, followed by **delayed optical reconstruction** once inflammation subsides to prevent amblyopia and restore near-perfect functional vision.
