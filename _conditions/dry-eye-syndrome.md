## Overview

Dry eye syndrome is a common and often chronic condition where the eyes don't produce enough tears or produce low-quality tears that evaporate too quickly. It affects 5-30% of adults, increasing with age.

While often uncomfortable, dry eye can be effectively managed with appropriate treatment tailored to the underlying cause.

---

## Types of Dry Eye

### Aqueous-Deficient Dry Eye
- **Cause:** Insufficient tear production (Sjögren's syndrome, lacrimal gland damage, hormonal changes)
- **Characteristics:** Dry sensation, grittiness, foreign body feeling
- **Treatment:** Artificial tears, punctal occlusion, prescription medications

### Evaporative Dry Eye
- **Cause:** Normal tear volume but rapid evaporation (meibomian gland dysfunction, blink abnormalities)
- **Characteristics:** Dry feeling, surface irritation, often worse later in day
- **Treatment:** Warm compresses, lid hygiene, omega-3 supplements

### Mixed Dry Eye
- **Cause:** Both insufficient production and rapid evaporation
- **Characteristics:** Variable symptoms
- **Treatment:** Multifaceted approach combining therapies

---

## Risk Factors

- **Age:** Increased incidence over 50
- **Female sex:** 2:1 female predominance (hormonal factors)
- **Menopause/Postmenopause:** Decreased estrogen reduces tear production
- **Systemic conditions:** Sjögren's, rheumatoid arthritis, lupus, scleroderma
- **Medications:** Antihistamines, anticholinergics, beta-blockers, antidepressants, isotretinoin
- **Screen time:** Reduced blinking during computer/phone use
- **Contact lens wear:** Increased tear evaporation
- **Chemical or thermal burns:** Prior eye injury
- **Graft-versus-host disease:** Post-bone marrow transplant

## Symptoms

### Mild to Moderate
- Dry, sandy, gritty feeling
- Burning or stinging sensation
- Mild blurring of vision
- Fluctuating vision throughout the day
- Foreign body sensation

### Severe
- Constant eye pain
- Significant vision reduction
- Photophobia (light sensitivity)
- Difficulty with daily activities
- Corneal surface damage

---

## Diagnosis

### Patient History
- Symptom onset and triggers
- Screen time and environmental exposures
- Medications and systemic diseases
- Contact lens use
- Previous eye injuries

### Clinical Tests

**Tear Osmolarity Test**
- Measures tear salt concentration
- Abnormal if > 308 mOsm/kg

**Schirmer Test**
- Measures tear production over 5 minutes
- Abnormal if < 5mm wetting

**Tear Breakup Time (TBUT)**
- Measures tear film stability
- Abnormal if < 5 seconds

**Ocular Surface Staining**
- Fluorescein stain: Highlights damaged epithelial cells
- Rose Bengal stain: Stains devitalized cells

**Meibomography**
- Thermal imaging of meibomian gland structure
- Identifies gland loss or obstruction

## Treatment Options

### Lifestyle Modifications (First-Line)
- **Screen breaks:** 20-20-20 rule (every 20 min, look 20 feet away, 20 seconds)
- **Humidity:** Use humidifier; avoid air conditioning/heating directly on face
- **Blinking exercises:** Conscious blinking during screen time
- **Hydration:** Increase fluid intake

### Topical Treatments
- **Artificial tears:** Frequent use (may need 4-8x daily)
- **Preservative-free formulations:** Reduce irritation from preservatives
- **Lipid-based drops:** For evaporative dry eye

### Eyelid Hygiene
- **Warm compresses:** 10-15 minutes daily to loosen meibomian gland plugs
- **Lid massage:** Gentle massage to express meibomian gland secretions
- **Mechanical cleaners:** Tea tree oil or baby shampoo solutions

### Nutritional Supplements
- **Omega-3 fatty acids:** 1000-2000 mg daily; improves tear film quality
- **Vitamin A:** Supports corneal epithelial health
- **Vitamin D:** Reduced levels associated with dry eye

### Punctal Occlusion
- **Temporary plugs:** Silicone; removed after 2 weeks trial
- **Permanent plugs:** Collagen or silicone; last 6-12+ months
- **Cautery:** Thermal or radiofrequency sealing of punctal opening

### Prescription Medications
- **Cyclosporine (Restasis):** Reduces inflammation; increases tear production
- **Lifitegrast (Xiidra):** Reduces surface inflammation
- **Doxycycline:** Anti-inflammatory; improves meibomian gland function

### Advanced Treatments
- **Intense Pulsed Light (IPL) Therapy:** Treats meibomian gland dysfunction
- **Meibomian Gland Expression:** Mechanical unblocking under anesthesia
- **Scleral Contact Lenses:** Vault the cornea; reduce tear evaporation

## Prevention & Long-Term Management

### Daily Routine
- Consistent artificial tear use (don't wait until symptomatic)
- Screen breaks and blinking exercises
- Warm compresses and lid hygiene
- Omega-3 supplement intake

### Environmental Control
- Avoid direct air conditioning/heating
- Use humidifier in dry climates
- Wear protective eyewear in windy conditions
- UV sunglasses outdoors

### Monitoring
- Annual eye exams (or more frequently if severe)
- Report any worsening or new symptoms
- Adjust medications/treatments as needed
