## What Is Glaucoma Laser Therapy?

Glaucoma laser therapy uses focused laser energy to improve aqueous fluid drainage or reduce production, thereby lowering intraocular pressure. It serves as an intermediate step between medications and surgery.

---

## Types of Glaucoma Laser

### Selective Laser Trabeculoplasty (SLT) — Preferred
**Mechanism:** Stimulates trabecular meshwork cells to improve drainage

**Laser:** Q-switched Nd:YAG laser (532 nm wavelength)

**Technique:**
- Selective targeting of pigmented trabecular meshwork cells
- Does NOT cause collateral thermal damage
- Repeatable; preserves laser-naive sites

**Effectiveness:**
- Average IOP reduction: 20-30%
- Success rate: 60-70% achieve clinically significant reduction
- Duration: 2-5 years average; wide individual variation

**Advantages:**
- Non-destructive (can be repeated)
- No scarring of trabeculum
- Faster onset (2-4 weeks for maximum effect)
- Can be used with all glaucoma types
- Bridge therapy while medications adjusted

**Indications:**
- Ocular hypertension or early open-angle glaucoma
- Inadequate IOP control on medications
- Medication intolerance
- As primary therapy in some cases
- Angle-closure glaucoma (after iridotomy)

---

### Argon Laser Trabeculoplasty (ALT) — Older Technology
**Mechanism:** Thermal burns to trabecular meshwork improve drainage

**Laser:** Argon laser (514 nm green or 488 nm blue wavelength)

**Technique:**
- Focal thermal burns across trabecular meshwork
- Creates scarring and collagen remodeling

**Effectiveness:**
- Average IOP reduction: 20-25%
- Success rate: 50% initially; decreases over time
- Duration: 2-5 years; success drops with repeat

**Disadvantages:**
- Creates permanent trabecular damage/scarring
- Cannot be repeated effectively (laser-burned areas still burned)
- Delayed healing (4-6 weeks for maximum effect)
- Higher risk of complications
- Generally replaced by SLT

**Current Role:**
- Historical comparison for SLT
- Rarely used now; SLT superior in all respects
- Reserved when SLT failed or unavailable

## Before Your Laser Treatment

### Pre-Treatment Evaluation
- **Gonioscopy:** Examine drainage angle
- **Baseline IOP:** Establish starting pressure
- **Visual field:** If indicated (advanced glaucoma)
- **Optic nerve photography:** Baseline for comparison

### Preparation
- Dilate pupils with drops (takes 20-30 minutes)
- Apply topical anesthesia
- Miotic agents optional (help angle visualization)
- Informed consent; discuss risks/benefits

### What to Expect
- Dim room with specialized laser equipment
- Contact lens placed on eye (may feel pressure)
- Bright light flashes during treatment
- Hearing laser "clicks" or sounds
- Possible mild discomfort/pressure sensation

### Timeline
- Check-in: 10-15 minutes
- Preparation: 20-30 minutes
- Laser treatment: 10-20 minutes
- Observation: 15-30 minutes
- **Total visit: 1-1.5 hours**

---

## The SLT Procedure

### Step-by-Step
1. **Positioning:** Sit at laser microscope
2. **Contact lens application:** Gonioscopy lens placed on eye with solution
3. **Alignment:** Surgeon positions laser beam on trabecular meshwork
4. **Laser pulses:** 50-100 individual laser spots applied across trabeculum
5. **Blanching response:** Watch for appropriate tissue response
6. **Lens removal:** Carefully remove contact lens
7. **Observation:** Monitor for immediate pressure spike

### Sensation During Procedure
- Bright white or green flashes
- Pressure sensation from contact lens
- Possible brief warm sensation
- NO pain (eye anesthetized)

### Duration
- Actual laser application: 10-20 minutes
- Total time in laser room: 20-30 minutes

---

## After Your Laser Treatment

### Immediate Post-Treatment (First Hour)
- Vision may be slightly blurred (anesthesia wears off)
- Mild eye discomfort possible
- IOP checked 1 hour after treatment
- May experience brief IOP spike (usually resolves spontaneously)

### First Day-Week
- Take prescribed anti-inflammatory drops
- Avoid strenuous activity, heavy lifting
- No eye rubbing
- Mild eye irritation/redness normal
- Contact ophthalmologist if severe pain develops

### Follow-Up Visits
- **1 week:** Check IOP; assess response
- **2-4 weeks:** Maximum IOP lowering effect appears
- **8 weeks:** Confirm IOP target achieved
- **6 months:** Reassess need for additional treatment

## Expected Outcomes

### IOP Reduction Timeline
- **Immediately after:** IOP unchanged or slightly elevated (transient spike)
- **Day 1-3:** IOP begins to drop
- **Week 2-4:** Maximal effect typically achieved
- **Month 3:** Plateau level reached

### Probability of Success
- **Significant reduction (≥ 20%):** 60-70%
- **Modest reduction (10-19%):** 15-20%
- **Minimal/no response (< 10%):** 15-25%

### Predictors of Better Response
- Lower baseline IOP
- Pseudoexfoliation glaucoma (better response)
- Primary angle-closure glaucoma (good response)
- Pigmentation of trabecular meshwork (higher pigmentation = better response)

### Duration of Effect
- **1 year:** 80% maintain effect
- **3 years:** 60-70% maintain effect
- **5 years:** 50% maintain effect
- **Beyond 5 years:** Variable; some require retreat, others maintain indefinitely

---

## Potential Complications

### Minor (Common)
- **Transient IOP spike:** Usually resolves; rarely causes damage
- **Anterior chamber inflammation:** Mild; controlled with anti-inflammatory drops
- **Conjunctival blanching:** Appearance of bleached conjunctival patches
- **Mild discomfort:** Typically resolves with topical drops

### Major (Rare)
- **Peripheral anterior synechiae:** Angle damage (< 1%)
- **Trabecular damage:** Irreversible reduction in drainage (very rare)
- **Hyphema:** Bleeding into anterior chamber (< 1%)
- **Corneal abrasion:** Scratch from contact lens (rare)

### Systemic Complications
- Extremely rare with topical/laser treatment
- No significant systemic absorption

## Treatment Escalation

### If SLT Insufficient
**Options:**
1. Add or adjust medications
2. Repeat SLT (if > 2 years since last treatment)
3. Proceed to surgical treatment (trabeculectomy, glaucoma drainage device)

### Timing for Repeat SLT
- **First SLT:** Months 0-6 (if needed bilaterally, can do second eye)
- **Repeat SLT:** Usually not before 12-24 months (allows new tissue response)
- **Maximum:** Can repeat indefinitely; laser-naive sites always available

### When to Consider Surgery
- Target IOP not achieved on medications + SLT
- Progressive optic nerve damage despite adequate IOP control
- Medications/SLT causing significant side effects
- Patient preference for definitive treatment

---

## Long-Term Follow-Up

### Monitoring Schedule
- **First 3 months:** Monthly IOP checks
- **Stabilized patients:** Every 3-6 months
- **Advanced glaucoma:** Every 1-3 months

### Maintenance Plan
- Continue medications as prescribed
- Regular optic nerve photography
- Annual or more frequent visual field testing
- Report any vision changes

### Retreatment Criteria
- IOP creeping back above target (> 20% increase from lowest point)
- Evidence of progressive optic nerve damage
- > 2 years since last treatment
- Patient desire for laser before surgery
