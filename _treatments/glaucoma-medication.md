## What Is Glaucoma Medication?

Glaucoma medications are eye drops or oral medications designed to lower intraocular pressure by enhancing aqueous fluid drainage or reducing fluid production. These medications are the first-line treatment for most glaucoma patients.

---

## How Medications Work

### Mechanism 1: Increase Aqueous Outflow
- **Prostaglandin analogs:** Enhance uveoscleral (unconventional) drainage
- **Alpha-agonists:** Increase trabecular and uveoscleral drainage
- **Carbonic anhydrase inhibitors:** Increase trabecular outflow

### Mechanism 2: Decrease Aqueous Production
- **Beta-blockers:** Reduce ciliary body aqueous secretion
- **Carbonic anhydrase inhibitors:** Reduce aqueous production
- **Alpha-agonists:** Minor reduction in production

---

## Drug Classes & Specific Medications

### Prostaglandin Analogs (Most Effective Single Agent)
**Mechanism:** Enhance uveoscleral drainage

**Agents:**
- Latanoprost 0.005% (Xalatan) — Evening dosing
- Travoprost 0.004% (Travatan Z) — Evening dosing
- Bimatoprost 0.03% (Lumigan) — Evening dosing

**Effectiveness:** Lower IOP 25-35%

**Side Effects:**
- Iris darkening (darkens light-colored irises)
- Eyelash growth (hypertrichosis)
- Periocular pigmentation (darkening around eye)
- Conjunctival hyperemia (redness)

**Contraindications:** Caution in cystoid macular edema; may exacerbate herpes simplex

---

### Beta-Blockers
**Mechanism:** Reduce aqueous production

**Agents:**
- Timolol 0.5% (Timoptic) — Twice daily
- Betaxolol 1% (Betopic) — Twice daily
- Levobunolol 0.5% (Betagan) — Once or twice daily

**Effectiveness:** Lower IOP 20-25%

**Advantages:** Inexpensive; no darkening of iris

**Side Effects:**
- Systemic absorption: Bradycardia, hypotension, bronchospasm
- Fatigue, depression, sexual dysfunction
- Contraindicated in asthma, COPD, heart block

---

### Alpha-2 Agonists
**Mechanism:** Increase outflow + decrease production

**Agents:**
- Brimonidine 0.2% (Alphagan) — Twice or three times daily
- Apraclonidine 1% (Iopidine) — 3 times daily (short-term only)

**Effectiveness:** Lower IOP 15-25%

**Advantages:** Some cardioprotective effects

**Side Effects:**
- Allergic contact dermatitis (up to 30%)
- Tachyphylaxis (decreasing effectiveness over time)
- CNS effects: Drowsiness, headache

**Note:** Apraclonidine reserved for short-term use (allergy risk); brimonidine better for maintenance

---

### Carbonic Anhydrase Inhibitors (Topical)
**Mechanism:** Reduce aqueous production + increase outflow

**Agents:**
- Dorzolamide 2% (Trusopt) — Three times daily
- Brinzolamide 1% (Azopt) — Three times daily

**Effectiveness:** Lower IOP 15-20%

**Advantages:** Useful in combination with other agents

**Side Effects:**
- Transient blurred vision (brinzolamide > dorzolamide)
- Taste alteration (metallic taste)
- Well tolerated; few systemic effects due to topical application

---

### Rho Kinase Inhibitors (Newest Class)
**Mechanism:** Increase trabecular outflow; reduce episcleral venous pressure

**Agents:**
- Netarsudil 0.02% (Rhopressa) — Once or twice daily
- Netarsudil + latanoprost combination (Rocklatan)

**Effectiveness:** Lower IOP 20-25% (monotherapy); 30%+ with combination

**Advantages:** Novel mechanism; can be used with all other classes

**Side Effects:**
- Conjunctival hyperemia (mild redness; 50%)
- Corneal verticillata (benign; resolves after discontinuation)
- Generally well tolerated

---

### Carbonic Anhydrase Inhibitors (Systemic Oral)
**Mechanism:** Reduce aqueous production

**Agents:**
- Acetazolamide 500 mg (Diamox) — Twice daily (extended-release available)
- Methazolamide 25-50 mg — Twice daily

**Effectiveness:** Lower IOP 20-30%

**Use:** Reserved for acute angle-closure or when topical therapy insufficient

**Side Effects:**
- Paresthesias (tingling fingers/toes)
- Altered taste (carbonation taste)
- Kidney stone risk (maintain hydration)
- Hypokalemia (monitor electrolytes)

**Monitoring:** Annual electrolyte panels recommended

## Combination Therapy

### Common Combinations
- **Prostaglandin + Beta-blocker:** Additive effect on IOP lowering
- **Prostaglandin + Carbonic anhydrase inhibitor:** 30-40% IOP reduction possible
- **Triple therapy:** Prostaglandin + Beta-blocker + Alpha-agonist or CAI

### Convenient Combinations (Fixed)
- **Timolol + Dorzolamide** (Cosopt) — Convenient; reduces adherence burden
- **Timolol + Brimonidine** (Combigan) — Similar convenience
- **Netarsudil + Latanoprost** (Rocklatan) — Newest combination

### Instillation Spacing
- **Different medications:** Space instillation by 5 minutes
- **Reason:** Allows absorption; prevents washout
- **Common schedule:** 8 AM (prostaglandin), 1 PM (beta-blocker), 6 PM (alpha-agonist)

## Treatment Goals & Targets

### Target IOP Selection
**Based on:**
- Baseline IOP (higher baseline = lower target needed)
- Severity of glaucomatous damage (more damage = lower target)
- Age and life expectancy
- Rate of progression before treatment

### Example Targets
- **Early mild glaucoma:** 20-21 mmHg (25-30% reduction from baseline)
- **Moderate glaucoma:** 14-18 mmHg (30-50% reduction)
- **Advanced glaucoma:** 12-14 mmHg (50%+ reduction)

### Monitoring Success
- **1 month:** IOP check; assess side effects
- **3 months:** Confirm target pressure achieved
- **6-12 months:** Annual or biannual IOP checks; visual field testing

## Adherence & Administration

### Proper Eye Drop Instillation
1. Wash hands
2. Tilt head back; look up
3. Pull down lower eyelid
4. Place drop in conjunctival sac (NOT on cornea)
5. Close eye gently (do NOT squeeze)
6. Occlude lacrimal drainage (press inner corner for 2-3 minutes)
7. Wipe away excess

### Why Occlusion Matters
- Reduces systemic absorption 25-50%
- Decreases side effects
- Improves efficacy

### Adherence Strategies
- Use reminder apps or alarms
- Pill organizers/drop reminders
- Link drops to daily routine (morning/evening)
- Regular follow-up appointments
- Discuss barriers to adherence

## Medication Switching & Escalation

### When to Switch
- Ineffective at lowering IOP
- Intolerable side effects
- Contraindication develops

### When to Add a Second Agent
- Monotherapy insufficient (usually after 4-6 weeks)
- Target IOP not reached
- Good tolerance (few side effects)

### Typical Escalation
```
Month 1-2:   Monotherapy (prostaglandin, usually)
Month 2-4:   Assess response; add second agent if needed
Month 4-8:   Combination therapy trial; optimize dosing
Month 8+:    Laser or surgery if target not achieved on 2-3 medications
```

## Systemic Considerations

### Medication Interactions
- **Beta-blockers + Verapamil:** Risk of severe bradycardia
- **Carbonic anhydrase inhibitors + Topiramate:** Additive side effects
- **Alpha-agonists + MAOI antidepressants:** Hypertensive crisis risk

### Comorbidity Considerations
- **Asthma/COPD:** Avoid beta-blockers; use prostaglandins, alpha-agonists, or CAI
- **Heart block:** Avoid beta-blockers
- **Kidney disease:** Caution with systemic CAI
- **Pregnancy:** Avoid prostaglandins (teratogenic); use beta-blockers or brimonidine

---

## Long-Term Management

### Medication Changes Over Time
- **Effectiveness may wane:** Prostaglandins sometimes lose efficacy over years (tachyphylaxis rare)
- **Side effects accumulate:** May need switches
- **New options available:** Netarsudil represents newer class
- **Systemic changes:** Aging, new comorbidities may alter tolerability

### Maintenance Schedule
- **First year:** Monthly or every 3 months
- **Stable patients:** Every 6-12 months
- **Advanced glaucoma:** Every 3-6 months

### When to Consider Surgery/Laser
- Target IOP not achieved on 3+ medications
- Intolerable side effects limiting options
- Progressive optic nerve damage despite adequate IOP
- Poor adherence limiting medical control
