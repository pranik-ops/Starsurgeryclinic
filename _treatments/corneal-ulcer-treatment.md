## What Is Corneal Ulcer Treatment?

Treatment of corneal ulcers requires aggressive antimicrobial therapy combined with supportive measures to promote healing and prevent complications like perforation, scarring, and vision loss.

The specific treatment depends on ulcer size, depth, location, causative organism (if identified), and response to initial therapy.

---

## Preliminary Evaluation

### Initial Assessment
- **Visual acuity:** Establish baseline; often reduced significantly
- **Slit lamp examination:** Assess ulcer location, size, depth, edges
- **Fluorescein staining:** Outline ulcer margins; identify infiltration
- **Gram stain and culture:** Identify organism (bacterial, fungal, viral, parasitic)
- **Anterior chamber exam:** Rule out iritis or hypopyon
- **Intraocular pressure:** Elevated pressure may develop

### Timeline to Treatment
- **Within 24 hours:** Culture results may be preliminary
- **Within 48-72 hours:** Organism identification and sensitivity results available
- **First 24 hours:** Empiric treatment initiated before culture results

---

## Immediate Treatment (First 24-48 Hours)

### Topical Antibiotics (Empiric)
**Goal:** Cover most common organisms (S. aureus, P. aeruginosa, Streptococcus)

**Fortified Antibiotics (initial 24-48 hours):**
- **Cefazolin 50 mg/mL:** Broad spectrum gram-positive coverage
- **Gatifloxacin 0.5%:** Gram-positive and gram-negative coverage
- **Tobramycin 0.3% + Gentamicin:** Gram-negative coverage

**Dosing:** Every 1-2 hours for first 24-48 hours (aggressive)

### Antifungal Coverage (if fungal suspected)
- **Natamycin 5%:** Most effective for fungal ulcers
- **Dosing:** Every 1-2 hours

### Antiviral Coverage (if HSV suspected)
- **Acyclovir ointment 3%:** For HSV keratitis
- **Trifluridine 1% drops:** Alternative for HSV
- **Dosing:** Every 2-3 hours

### Supportive Therapy
- **Cycloplegic agents:** Reduce pain from ciliary spasm
  - Cyclopentolate 1% every 6-8 hours
- **Lubricating drops:** Promote healing and comfort
  - Preservative-free artificial tears every 1-2 hours
- **Eye protection:** Sterile eye patch or bandage lens
- **Oral analgesia:** Pain management (acetaminophen, ibuprofen, or prescription opioids for severe pain)

---

## Subsequent Management (After 48-72 Hours)

### Organism-Directed Therapy
**Based on culture and sensitivity results:**

**Bacterial Ulcer:**
- Switch from fortified drops to less concentrated drops
- Single antibiotic with organism sensitivity
- Continue every 1-2 hours initially, then taper

**Fungal Ulcer:**
- Continue natamycin (or switch based on sensitivity)
- Add oral antifungal (voriconazole, fluconazole, itraconazole)
- May require topical steroid adjunctively (carefully, once infection controlled)

**Viral Ulcer (HSV):**
- Continue topical antiviral (acyclovir, trifluridine)
- Add systemic antiviral (acyclovir 400 mg 5x daily, or valacyclovir 500 mg 3x daily)
- Topical steroids can be carefully introduced after 3-5 days of antiviral therapy

**Parasitic Ulcer (rare):**
- Treat based on organism identification
- Often requires surgical intervention

### Assessment & Modifications
- **Daily exams:** Assess ulcer response to therapy
- **Modify therapy:** Based on response and lab results
- **Taper treatment:** As ulcer improves
- **Monitor for perforation:** Risk especially at 3-5 days of active disease

---

## Adjunctive Measures

### Soft Contact Lens (Bandage Lens)
- **Indication:** Mechanical protection; promotes epithelial healing
- **Type:** Silicone hydrogel (better oxygen transmission)
- **Care:** Must be managed by ophthalmologist; removed and cleaned daily
- **Duration:** Typically 5-14 days

### Topical Steroids (Controversial)
- **Indication:** Reduce inflammation once infection controlled (after 3-5 days of antimicrobials for bacterial; later for fungal)
- **Caution:** Risk of geographic or progressive ulcers if steroids used too early
- **Dosing:** Prednisolone acetate 1% every 4-6 hours initially, then taper
- **Monitoring:** Frequent exams essential; discontinue if worsening

### Systemic Therapy
- **Oral antibiotics:** For severe or non-healing ulcers (ciprofloxacin, doxycycline)
- **Anti-inflammatories:** NSAIDs for pain management
- **Nutritional support:** Vitamin C, vitamin A promote healing

## Surgical Intervention (If Needed)

### Indications for Surgery
- **Perforation:** Full-thickness corneal hole (emergency)
- **Non-healing ulcer:** After 2-4 weeks of medical therapy
- **Large ulcer:** Approaching 50% stromal depth
- **Immune compromise:** Fungal or atypical organisms

### Surgical Options

**Debridement:**
- Remove necrotic, infected tissue
- Allows better antibiotic penetration

**Conjunctival Flap:**
- Cover ulcer with conjunctival flap
- Promotes healing; used for non-healing ulcers
- Temporary (dissolves in 4-6 weeks) or permanent

**Corneal Patch Graft:**
- Suture intact corneal graft over ulcer
- For large perforations or significant stromal loss
- Allows refractive correction later

**Corneal Transplant:**
- Full-thickness transplant for vision
- Reserved for severe scarring or large defects
- Performed after infection fully treated

**Cyanoacrylate Glue (Tissue Adhesive):**
- For perforations < 2mm
- Seals perforation while healing occurs
- Requires close monitoring

## Expected Healing Timeline

| Timepoint | Status | Findings |
|-----------|--------|----------|
| **24 hours** | Early response | Ulcer may appear worse (infiltration) initially; pain control achieved |
| **48-72 hours** | Organism identified | Culture results guide therapy changes |
| **1 week** | Response evaluation | Small ulcers show epithelialization beginning; pain subsiding |
| **2-3 weeks** | Active healing | Most uncomplicated ulcers epithelialized; antibiotics tapered |
| **4-6 weeks** | Late healing | Epithelial defect closed; corneal opacity stabilizing |
| **2-3 months** | Remodeling | Corneal haze may improve; vision gradually clarifies |
| **6-12 months** | Final outcome | Vision stabilizes; residual scarring apparent by 6 months |

## Postoperative/Post-Treatment Care

### Drop Taper Schedule (Typical)
```
Weeks 1-2:  Every 1-2 hours (awake)
Weeks 3-4:  Every 2-3 hours (awake)
Weeks 5-6:  Every 3-4 hours + at bedtime
Weeks 7+:   Every 4-6 hours, then discontinue
```

### Follow-Up Exams
- **Days 1-3:** Daily evaluation
- **Weeks 1-4:** Twice weekly
- **Weeks 4-8:** Weekly
- **Weeks 8-12:** Bi-weekly
- **After healing:** Monthly for 6 months

### Visual Rehabilitation
- **Refraction:** Once corneal remodeling complete (2-3 months post-healing)
- **Glasses/contacts:** Correct residual refractive error
- **Low vision assessment:** If significant scarring causes reduced vision

## Potential Complications

- **Corneal scarring:** Reduces vision; may require transplant
- **Corneal neovascularization:** Abnormal vessel growth from scarring
- **Astigmatism:** Irregular scarring causes refractive error
- **Corneal perforation:** Risk period: 3-5 days of active disease
- **Endophthalmitis:** Intraocular infection (rare; requires vitrectomy)
- **Secondary glaucoma:** Inflammation-related pressure elevation

---

## Prevention of Recurrence

### Contact Lens Safety
- Strict hygiene protocols
- Appropriate lens cleaning solutions
- Regular lens replacement
- Avoid overnight wear

### Eye Protection
- Protective eyewear during work/sports
- Avoid eye trauma
- Prompt treatment of minor scratches

### Systemic Measures
- Treatment of rosacea (associated with bacterial keratitis)
- Management of autoimmune disease
- Vaccination against HSV (future developments)
