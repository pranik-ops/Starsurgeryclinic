## What Is Anti-VEGF Therapy?

Anti-VEGF (Vascular Endothelial Growth Factor) therapy involves injecting medication directly into the vitreous cavity of the eye to block abnormal blood vessel growth and reduce fluid leakage. This promotes vision recovery in conditions like diabetic macular edema, wet age-related macular degeneration, and retinal vein occlusion.

---

## Mechanism of Action

**VEGF:** A signaling protein that promotes:
- New blood vessel formation (neovascularization)
- Increased vascular permeability (fluid leakage)
- Inflammation

**Anti-VEGF drugs block VEGF, preventing:**
- Abnormal vessel proliferation
- Macular edema (fluid accumulation)
- Vision-threatening complications

| Drug | Brand | Dosing | Characteristics |
|------|-------|--------|-----------------|
| Bevacizumab | Avastin | 1.25 mg/0.05 mL | Off-label; most affordable; shorter duration |

| Aflibercept | Eylea | 2 mg/0.05 mL | Newest; longer duration; extended dosing intervals possible |
| Brolucizumab | Beovu | 6 mg/0.05 mL | Newest; very long duration; emerging alternative |

## Before Your Procedure

### Preparation
- Dilate pupils with drops (takes 20-30 minutes)
- Prepare eye surface with povidone-iodine wash
- Obtain informed consent; discuss risks/benefits
- Topical anesthesia applied

### What to Expect
- Dim room and microscope setup
- Speculum to hold eyelids open
- Feeling of pressure during injection
- Brief flash of light or medication taste

### Timeline
- Check-in: 15 minutes
- Preparation: 20-30 minutes
- Injection: < 5 minutes
- Post-injection observation: 15-30 minutes
- **Total visit: 1-1.5 hours**

---

## The Injection Procedure

### Step-by-Step
1. **Anesthesia:** Topical drops numb the eye surface
2. **Antisepsis:** Povidone-iodine solution sterilizes ocular surface
3. **Speculum placement:** Instrument holds eyelids open
4. **Injection site:** 3.5-4mm posterior to corneal limbus (determined by eye size/anatomy)
5. **Injection:** Needle advances slowly into vitreous
6. **Medication delivery:** Drug injected slowly
7. **Needle withdrawal:** Careful removal; pressure applied to injection site
8. **Observation:** Monitor for complications

### Sensation During Procedure
- Pressure sensation (normal)
- Possible brief discomfort if touching retina
- No pain (eye anesthetized)
- May hear clicking sounds (scleral entry)

---

## After Your Injection

### Immediate Post-Injection (First Hour)
- Vision may be blurred (temporary)
- Floaters or dark spots (normal; medication in vitreous)
- Some soreness around injection site
- Avoid rubbing eye

### First Week
- Continue prescribed eyedrops
- Avoid strenuous activity, heavy lifting
- No swimming or water sports
- Report any pain, vision changes, or discharge immediately

### Ongoing Care
- Follow-up exam in 3-7 days
- Additional exams per treatment schedule
- Continue systemic medications (diabetes, BP control)
- Monthly injections initially; then PRN based on OCT

---

## Treatment Schedule

### Diabetic Macular Edema (DME)
- **Months 1-3:** Monthly injections (3 total)
- **Month 4+:** PRN (as-needed) based on OCT and symptoms
- **Typical:** 6-12 injections per year

### Wet Age-Related Macular Degeneration
- **Months 1-3:** Monthly injections (3 total)
- **Month 4+:** PRN or fixed every 8 weeks (aflibercept)
- **Typical:** 8-12 injections per year

### Retinal Vein Occlusion
- **Months 1-3:** Monthly injections
- **Month 4+:** PRN based on response
- **Typical:** 8-10 injections per year

## Expected Outcomes

### Vision Improvement
- **2-4 weeks:** OCT shows edema reduction
- **1-3 months:** Vision improvement becomes apparent
- **3-6 months:** Maximum vision recovery usually achieved
- **Success rate:** 60-70% gain vision or stabilize; 30-40% modest improvement

### Visual Acuity Results (DME Example)
- **Improved:** 30-40% (2+ lines on eye chart)
- **Stabilized:** 30-40% (maintained, prevented further loss)
- **Declined:** 20-30% (despite treatment; may need alternative therapy)

### Macular Edema Reduction
- **OCT Central Subfield Thickness:**
  - Baseline: Often > 300 microns
  - Target: < 250 microns (some eyes < 200)
  - Improvement: Usually visible by 3-4 weeks

## Potential Complications

### Minor (Common)
- **Floaters:** Medication particles in vitreous (temporary)
- **Transient vision blur:** Clears within hours
- **Subconjunctival hemorrhage:** Blood under conjunctiva; resolves 2-4 weeks
- **Injection site irritation:** Brief discomfort

### Major (Rare, < 1%)
- **Endophthalmitis:** Intraocular infection (0.01-0.05% per injection)
- **Retinal detachment:** Separation of retina (rare)
- **Retinal tear:** Hole in retina (rare)
- **Hemorrhage:** Bleeding into vitreous or subretinal (rare)
- **Cataract:** May accelerate if present (long-term)

### Systemic Risks (Theoretical)
- **Myocardial infarction:** Rare systemic absorption
- **Stroke:** Rare; bevacizumab carries slightly higher risk than ranibizumab
- **Thromboembolic events:** Rare; anti-VEGF may promote clotting

---

## Long-Term Management

### Maintenance Therapy
- **Regular OCT imaging:** Assess edema status
- **Monthly or quarterly exams:** Determine PRN need
- **Visual acuity testing:** Track functional vision
- **Systemic disease control:** Diabetes, hypertension management critical

### Treatment Intervals
- If stable on PRN: Can extend to every 8-12 weeks
- If recurrent edema: May need monthly maintenance
- If non-responsive: Consider switching agents or adding laser

### Duration of Treatment
- **Typically lifelong** for chronic conditions (DME, wet AMD, RVO)
- **May be able to discontinue** if edema fully resolved for 6+ months (rare)
- **Needs reassessment** if vision stable but not improving further
